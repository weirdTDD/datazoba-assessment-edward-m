2019 — The Foundation

Afrobeats is a regional genre. The world isn't listening yet.

Nigeria dominates. Ghana, South Africa and Côte d'Ivoire contribute steadily. But international streams are barely a rounding error — the diaspora knows, and almost nobody else does.

~18M Nigeria monthly streams · Jan 2019
2020 — Quiet Acceleration

COVID locks people inside. The algorithm finds new ears.

Global streaming surges across every genre. But something different is happening with Afrobeats — TikTok's early adopters are circulating Nigerian and Ghanaian tracks through sound communities the industry hasn't mapped yet. International numbers begin to inch.

2021 — Viral Breakout

Essence. Peru. City Boys. The wave breaks.

WizKid's Essence becomes the song of the summer — not just in Lagos, but in London, Toronto and São Paulo. TikTok is now the distribution engine. International streams double in a single year. The genre's trajectory bends permanently upward.

4.2B TikTok videos · Essence alone
2022 — Mainstream Entry

Calm Down. Last Last. Afrobeats enters the charts.

Rema's Calm Down charts across Europe. Burna Boy is now a Grammy winner. Labels begin signing Afrobeats acts in earnest. The gap between African and international streams narrows for the first time — not because Africa slows down, but because the rest of the world catches up.

2023 — Tyla's Year

Water. One song. Fifty-five countries charted.

Tyla's Water peaks at #1 globally. It is no longer a story about a genre breaking through — it is a story about a genre that has fully embedded itself in global pop infrastructure. The question is no longer "will Afrobeats go global?" It already has.

2024 — The New Normal

The genre is no longer "emerging." It never will be again.

International Afrobeats streams are on track to exceed African domestic streams for the first time. The cultural arbitrage is complete. A sound that was once described as "exotic" in Western trade press is now simply — music.
